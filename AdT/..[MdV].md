## 1. FORMAL AND PROCEDURAL DEFECTS

### 1.2. Defective Form: "Informe" vs. Formal Resolution
**Legal Basis:** LPACAP Art. 88.

The document is classified as an *"Informe"* (Report) in the signature table, yet it contains a final dispositive decision: *"no se considera técnicamente justificada la suspensión... manteniéndose las condiciones de aprovechamiento"*. 

An *informe técnico* is an instructional act, not a final act that puts an end to the administrative procedure. If this document is being used as the final resolution, it **confuses procedural phases** and violates the formal requirements of a *resolución* under LPACAP.

### 1.3. Omission of Appeal Information ("Pie de Recursos")
**Legal Basis:** LPACAP Art. 88.3 and Art. 40.2.

LPACAP Art. 88.3 requires that resolutions *"expresarán, además, los recursos que contra la misma procedan, órgano administrativo o judicial ante el que hubieran de presentarse y plazo para interponerlos"*. Art. 40.2 requires the same in the notification.

**The document completely omits this information.** This is a serious formal defect that:
- Violates the right to effective judicial protection (Art. 24 CE).
- May render the act anullable for violation of essential formal requirements.
- Could trigger the *silencio administrativo* regime if the act is not considered a valid express resolution.

### 1.4. Lack of Reference to Specific Legal Precepts
**Legal Basis:** LPACAP Art. 35.1 (motivation with reference to facts and legal grounds).

The document provides factual arguments but **fails to cite the specific legal articles** that empower the Administration to deny the suspension. It does not reference:
- The applicable provision of *Ley 9/1998, de Caza de La Rioja* (e.g., Art. 52.3 on varying hunting periods).
- *Ley 42/2007, del Patrimonio Natural y de la Biodiversidad* (e.g., Art. 65.3.d on temporary moratoriums).
- The *Orden Anual de Caza* provisions on urgent measures.
- The *Reglamento de Caza de La Rioja* (approved by Decreto 17/2004).

A resolution that limits subjective rights or interests without citing its legal basis is defectively motivated and vulnerable to annulment.

## 2. DEFECTS IN MOTIVATION AND REASONING

### 2.1. Stereotyped and Generic Motivation
**Legal Basis:** LPACAP Art. 35; STS 25/01/2006; STS 18/03/2015.

The document uses boilerplate arguments applicable to virtually any hunting suspension request:
- *"Las condiciones meteorológicas... constituyen factores ambientales que pueden influir... No obstante, la existencia de estas condiciones ambientales no permite concluir, por sí sola..."*
- *"Las capturas obtenidas... dependen no solo de la abundancia... sino también de la presión y del esfuerzo cinegético..."*

The Supreme Court has repeatedly held that **motivation cannot be stereotyped** (*motivación estereotipada*). The Administration must analyze the **specific circumstances of the case** (the 2026 summer heatwave, specific quail population data for La Rioja, etc.). The document reads like a template rather than a case-specific analysis.

### 2.2. Self-Contradictory Reasoning
The document simultaneously argues:
- That the petitioner **failed to provide** population censuses, abundance indices, productivity estimates, or mortality data.
- That the Administration **nonetheless concludes** that *"no se observan elementos técnicos que permitan acreditar que el aprovechamiento... vaya a comprometer su estado de conservación"*.

This is logically inconsistent: if there is no data, the Administration cannot assert that conservation is not at risk. It is making a positive claim (hunting is safe) based on an admitted absence of evidence. This violates the **principle of proper motivation**, which requires logical coherence between facts, law, and decision.

## 3. VIOLATION OF THE DUTY TO INVESTIGATE

### 3.1. Breach of LPACAP Art. 75 (Officiality of Instruction)
**Legal Basis:** LPACAP Art. 75.1.

LPACAP Art. 75.1 states: *"Los actos de instrucción necesarios para la determinación, conocimiento y comprobación de los hechos... se realizarán de oficio... por el órgano que tramite el procedimiento"*.

The document places the entire burden of proof on the petitioner:
- *"En particular, en el escrito presentado no se aportan resultados de censos poblacionales, índices de abundancia estandarizados, estimaciones de productividad, datos de supervivencia, evidencias de mortalidad extraordinaria ni otros parámetros demográficos que permitan acreditar una afección poblacional..."*

This is **not a valid administrative procedure**. The Administration cannot reject a request simply because the petitioner did not provide all scientific data. It has a **positive duty to investigate** (*deber de investigación*), especially in environmental matters where it holds institutional advantages and monitoring obligations.

The Administration should have:
- Conducted its own population surveys or reviewed existing ones.
- Requested additional data from the petitioner if needed (Art. 87, actuaciones complementarias).
- Consulted the *Consejo de Caza de La Rioja* (required by Ley 9/1998, Art. 52.3 for period variations/bans).

### 3.2. Failure to Conduct Complementary Investigations
**Legal Basis:** LPACAP Art. 87.

Before issuing a resolution, the competent organ may agree to *"actuaciones complementarias indispensables para resolver el procedimiento"*. Given the acknowledged lack of population data, the Administration should have either conducted complementary investigations or explained why they were unnecessary. It did neither.

## 4. VIOLATION OF THE PRECAUTIONARY PRINCIPLE

### 4.1. Inversion of the Precautionary Principle
**Legal Basis:** Principle 15 of the Rio Declaration; TFEU Art. 191.2; Spanish Constitution Art. 45; Ley 42/2007.

The precautionary principle operates when there is **scientific uncertainty** about environmental harm but **indicators of risk** exist. The document acknowledges:
- Elevated temperatures and reduced water availability during summer 2026.
- Historical decline in quail captures (though attributing it to hunting pressure).
- Quail is a migratory species with high interannual variability.

Yet it concludes: *"para justificar técnicamente una suspensión general del aprovechamiento sería necesario constatar que dichas circunstancias están provocando efectos biológicos relevantes... mortalidades anormales, una reducción extraordinaria de su capacidad de respuesta..."*

**This inverts the precautionary principle.** The principle requires that **lack of full scientific certainty shall not be used as a reason for postponing cost-effective measures to prevent environmental degradation**. By requiring proof of *"mortalidades anormales"* and *"reducción extraordinaria"* before acting, the Administration places an impossible burden on the petitioner and ignores its own duty to act preventively.

### 4.2. EU Law: Birds Directive 2009/147/EC
**Legal Basis:** Directive 2009/147/EC, Art. 7; TJUE Case C-435/92 (Association pour la protection des animaux sauvages).

The Common Quail (*Coturnix coturnix*) is a migratory bird covered by the EU Birds Directive. The European Commission has identified it as a species of conservation concern in Spain. The TJUE has ruled that in cases of uncertainty about the timing of bird migrations, Member States must apply the **stricter protective method** (C-435/92).

The document's approach—waiting for definitive proof of population collapse before restricting hunting—directly contradicts EU jurisprudence on the precautionary principle and sustainable hunting.

## 5. VIOLATIONS OF HUNTING AND ENVIRONMENTAL LEGISLATION

### 5.1. Ley 42/2007, del Patrimonio Natural y de la Biodiversidad
**Legal Basis:** Ley 42/2007, Art. 65.2 and 65.3.d.

- **Art. 65.2:** Hunting must be regulated so that *"queden garantizados la conservación y el fomento de las especies autorizadas"*. The Administration's decision to maintain hunting without verifying population status violates this guarantee.
- **Art. 65.3.d:** *"Se podrán establecer moratorias temporales o prohibiciones especiales cuando razones de orden biológico o sanitario lo aconsejen"*. The document interprets this restrictively, requiring absolute proof of biological harm. However, the law uses the term *"aconsejen"* (advise/recommend), which is precautionary and does not require certainty.

### 5.2. Ley 9/1998, de Caza de La Rioja
**Legal Basis:** Ley 9/1998, Art. 52.3; Art. 60.2.

- **Art. 52.3:** *"Cuando en determinadas zonas existan razones que así lo justifiquen, la Consejería competente, **oído el Consejo de Caza de La Rioja**, podrá variar los períodos hábiles... o establecer la veda total o parcial"*. The document does not mention whether the *Consejo de Caza* was consulted. If it was not, this is a procedural violation.
- **Art. 60.2:** Allows the *Consejería* to limit or prohibit hunting where there are *"indicios razonables"* of epizootics. While this article refers to diseases, it demonstrates that the regional legislator accepts precautionary action based on reasonable indicators, not just proven facts.

### 5.3. Orden Anual de Caza (Urgent Measures)
**Legal Basis:** Orden AGR/28/2019, Art. 17 (and equivalent provisions in subsequent annual orders).

The annual hunting orders in La Rioja typically include an article (e.g., Art. 17) allowing the *Dirección General de Medio Natural* to adopt *"medidas urgentes"* when meteorological, biological, or ecological conditions advise it, including:
- Modifying hunting periods.
- Establishing total or partial bans.
- Fixing capture limitations.

The document acknowledges adverse meteorological conditions but dismisses them as insufficient *"por sí solas"*. This ignores the **urgent measures clause** that specifically empowers the Administration to act precisely when such conditions arise.

## 6. BREACH OF THE PRINCIPLE OF CONGRUENCE

**Legal Basis:** LPACAP Art. 88.2.

Art. 88.2 states: *"En los procedimientos tramitados a solicitud del interesado, la resolución será congruente con las peticiones formuladas por éste"*.

The petitioner's request was based on:
1. Specific 2026 summer meteorological conditions (heat, drought).
2. Evolution of quail populations (with historical capture data).
3. The need for precautionary suspension.

The resolution:
- Does not specifically address the **2026 summer conditions** with its own technical data (temperature records, drought indices, habitat moisture levels).
- Dismisses capture data as merely an exploitation indicator, but does not provide its own alternative population data.
- Does not explain why the precautionary principle does not apply to this specific case.

This lack of specific engagement with the petitioner's arguments violates the principle of congruence.

## 7. POTENTIAL CONFLICT WITH THE "PROYECTO COTURNIX" AND STATE OBLIGATIONS

**Legal Basis:** MAPA/Comité de Caza; European Commission assessment.

The Spanish Ministry of Agriculture (MAPA) has identified the Common Quail as a species of EU conservation concern. The *Proyecto Coturnix* (citizen science project) was initiated precisely because the European Commission demanded better management of quail populations. 

By refusing to suspend hunting based on admitted uncertainty, La Rioja's Administration may be:
- Failing to cooperate with Spain's national strategy for sustainable quail management.
- Potentially exposing Spain to infraction proceedings under EU law (Art. 258 TFEU) if the hunting season contributes to population decline.
